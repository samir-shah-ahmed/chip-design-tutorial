FPGA clocks are a network, not a signal you can AND with a button. Gating clk with a LUT output produces a clock that can glitch, and the timer in nextpnr will not treat that net as the oscillator you constrained.

Prefer one of these:

  • A clock enable into the flip-flop: the clock pin stays on the global net, and the register ignores edges when the enable is low. This lesson builds that enable.
  • A PLL (Gowin rPLL) when you truly need another frequency. The Tang Nano 20K also has an MS5351 whose outputs land on FPGA pins; that part is configured by the onboard debugger, which the 20K track covers. A PLL is still a hard block, instantiated as an IP or a primitive, not as behavioral divides on the clock wire.

Exercise: Pulse ce for one clock every 4 cycles. Over 8 clocks after reset the testbench should count exactly 2 pulses.
