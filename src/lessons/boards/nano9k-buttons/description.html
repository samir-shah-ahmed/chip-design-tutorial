A mechanical button chatters. Sampling the pin directly in an FSM will see several presses. The circuit here is the one used on these boards: two flip-flops to enter the clock domain, then a counter that only accepts the level after it has been stable.

On the Tang Nano 9K the Sipeed/Lushay UART article says the onboard button is pulled up to 1.8 V through 10 kΩ and reads low when pressed. Their CST names that button btn1 on pin 3. Sipeed's FAQ says key S2 is wired to JTAGSEL_N; the official HDMI example constrains resetn to pin 4. Do not use S2 as a casual user button if you also need JTAG.

On the Tang Nano 20K, the LiteX board file (a secondary source, not the Sipeed blink article) places buttons on pins 88 and 87. Confirm those against the 20K schematic before you depend on them. The Sipeed blink article itself documents the 27 MHz clock on pin 4 and LED0 on pin 15, and does not list the buttons.

IO_LOC "btn1" 3;
IO_PORT "btn1" IO_TYPE=LVCMOS18;

Exercise: Raise pressed only after s1 has been low for STABLE clocks. The testbench holds the button and expects PASS pressed. STABLE is 3 so the simulation is short. On a 27 MHz board, tens of milliseconds is a more realistic threshold.
