Power, area, and timing pull against each other, and the RTL is where a lot of that bargain is made.

  • Timing wants more pipeline registers and more parallelism. That spends area and, if the extra registers toggle, power.
  • Area wants sharing: one multiplier used on two cycles instead of two multipliers. That spends latency.
  • Dynamic power in CMOS is the energy of charging capacitance: it grows with capacitance, with the square of voltage, and with how often nodes switch. A register that reloads the same value every clock still clocks its pin. A clock enable, or a clock-gate cell from the standard-cell library, stops that. Do not build the gate by ANDing the clock in RTL. The library cell is designed so the clock does not glitch; a LUT or a random AND gate is not.
  • Leakage is the power you pay when nothing switches. Fewer cells and higher-threshold libraries are the usual levers. RTL cannot see threshold voltage.

On an FPGA the same enable saves routing and LUT toggles, but you cannot pick a high-Vt flip-flop, and you cannot change the supply. The ASIC bargain is wider because the library and the PDK are part of the design.
