Signoff is the set of checks that happen after the layout exists. Two of them compare geometry to intent:

  • DRC (design rule check) — widths, spacings, enclosures, and density on every layer. The rules come from the PDK. Sky130 and SG13G2 publish theirs; a commercial foundry's rule deck is usually under NDA. KLayout and Magic are the open tools people run these decks with.
  • LVS (layout versus schematic) — extract transistors from the drawn shapes and prove they are the same netlist synthesis produced. A short or an open that DRC missed shows up here, as does a cell that was placed but never connected.

Timing signoff re-runs STA on the extracted parasitics, often at several process-voltage-temperature corners. Power signoff checks IR drop and electromigration on the power grid. None of that is what the testbench below does.

The testbench is the idea LVS is protecting, at RTL: a specification (y = ~(a & b)) and an implementation (a NAND2 cell) must agree on every input. Real LVS compares extracted devices, not two Verilog functions. Passing this testbench is not a DRC or an LVS result.
